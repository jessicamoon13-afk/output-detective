Detective’s Quick Guide

Use this guide when you are unsure what a value is telling you.

Means & SDs

Means describe the center or average pattern. Standard deviations describe variability around the mean.

p-values

Compare p to alpha, usually .05. p < .05 suggests statistical significance; p ≥ .05 does not.

t and F values

These are test statistics for group comparison models. Interpret them with their p-values and direction/pattern.

R and R²

R describes relationship strength. R² describes the proportion of variance explained by a regression model.

Coefficients

A coefficient describes the predicted change in the outcome for a one-unit increase in the predictor.

Confidence Intervals

CIs provide a range of plausible values for an estimate. For mean differences and slopes, intervals crossing zero require caution.

ICC, SEM, MDC

ICC describes reliability, SEM describes typical measurement error, and MDC describes the threshold for change beyond error.

Professional Language

Avoid “proved,” “guaranteed,” and causal claims unless the study design supports them.

How to Play

You are the detective. Each card gives you statistical evidence: a small output table, result statement, or scenario. Choose the interpretation that best matches the evidence.

  • Find the Evidence: identify which statistic answers the question.
  • Interpret the Output: translate output into responsible plain-language meaning.
  • Spot the Problem: identify overclaims, missing context, or common interpretation mistakes.

After each answer, read the feedback carefully. The goal is not speed; the goal is accurate statistical reasoning.
